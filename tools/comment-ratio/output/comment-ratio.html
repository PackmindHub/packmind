The flow

1. What gets written

For each fortnight — models ship mid-month, so a monthly step would not separate the weeks before a release from the weeks after it — the net diff between the two boundaries: of the lines added, what share is comment. Measuring the net rather than summing each commit means the chart counts what a period left behind, not everything typed during it; on this repository the two differ by 5 to 37% in volume but by less than half a point in ratio, because churn lands on comment and code lines alike. The ratio over the whole codebase, by contrast, moves slowly: it is dominated by what was written months ago.

Comment ratio of added lines — net diff between period boundaries

Each point is a single git diff between the last commit before the period opens and the last commit before it closes, not a sum over the period's commits. A line written on the 3rd and rewritten on the 9th of the same fortnight counts once, in its final form; a line added and removed inside the period does not count at all. The fortnightly series cannot separate the week before a release from the week after it. Day by day, the switch and the change land together. Counting only days that carry more than 300 lines, the median Opus day before the release is at 1.6% and the median after it at 14.7%. The two sets do overlap at their edges — 9 Jul reaches 13.7% on the old model and 17 Aug sits at 2.9% on the new one — so no single day proves anything; it is the bulk that moves. And it keeps moving: over the first half of the post-release stretch the median Opus 5 day is at 10.1%, over the second half 23.0%. Whatever changed did not settle on the day the model shipped. Opus 5 shipped on a Friday, so its first working days are the Monday and Tuesday that follow — and the two instruction changes that might otherwise explain the move landed on the Wednesday and Thursday after that, once the rise had already started.

How it is measured

Method and limits

Every line of a file goes into exactly one bucket — blank, code, or comment — and the ratio is then comments over non-blank lines:

comment ratio = comment lines comment lines + code lines

Blank lines count neither in the numerator nor in the denominator. A line is a comment only when every non-whitespace character on it belongs to one: const a = 1; // why is therefore code. That is the convention cloc uses.

Two sets of lines feed into it, and that is the only difference between the report's two measures. The flow takes the lines added during a period; the stock takes every line of every file at a date. Worked on the 1 Sept → 15 Sept 26 period, which you can find in the table above:

17.9% = 6,276 6,276 + 28,799
  • Comments are located with the official TypeScript parser rather than a regular expression, so strings, template literals, regex literals and JSX text containing // are not mistaken for comments. The JSX idiom {'{/* ... */}'} counts as a comment, on every line it spans.
  • .d.ts files are excluded. Renames are detected, so moving a file does not look like new code.
  • The repository's public history starts on 5 September 2025 with an import of an already existing codebase. That first delta is not a period of development and is left out of the charts.
  • The trailer records the model of the session that produced a commit, not necessarily the author of every line in it. Pull requests are squashed on merge, so one trailer covers one pull request.
  • Commits from 18 September 2026 onwards are excluded (--until 2026-09-18): a deliberate comment-rewriting pass landed that day, and it would have accounted for nearly half of the final period's added comment lines while removing far more.
  • A modified line reaches git as a removal plus an addition, so "added lines" includes rewrites. The ratio answers "when this code was written, what share was comment", not "how much net documentation was produced".
  • Commits with no trailer (most of 2025) are of unknown attribution, not "human".
